# Energy/Environmental Engineering Abstract Example

**Journal**: Applied Energy (Elsevier)
**Paper type**: Life-cycle assessment + building energy simulation
**Topic**: Whole-life carbon and energy performance of net-zero carbon retrofits

---

## Example Abstract

Whole-building decarbonization of the existing built environment is critical to
meeting national net-zero commitments, yet the interaction between operational
carbon reduction and embodied carbon from deep retrofit measures remains poorly
quantified at the portfolio scale.
This study presents a coupled life-cycle assessment (LCA) and building energy
simulation (BES) framework applied to a representative sample of 120 UK residential
buildings spanning five construction archetypes and three climate zones (ASHRAE
zones 4A–5C).
EnergyPlus v23.1 was calibrated against 24 months of smart-meter data
(calibration CVRMSE = 7.8%, MBE = −1.2%, within ASHRAE 14 thresholds) and used
to generate whole-building energy use intensity (EUI) predictions for eight retrofit
scenarios.
Embodied carbon was quantified using ecoinvent v3.10 with the ReCiPe 2016 midpoint
(H) method and GWP100 characterization factors from IPCC AR6.
Results show that heat pump adoption combined with external wall insulation (U-value
≤0.15 W/m²K) delivers the highest whole-life carbon reduction (58 ± 6% relative
to the 1990 baseline) with a levelized cost of carbon saving (LCCS) of
£47–£63/tCO₂e, compared to £110–£180/tCO₂e for solar PV alone.
Embodied carbon from insulation materials contributes 8–14% of the 50-year whole-life
carbon budget, indicating that low-carbon material selection is a secondary but
non-negligible lever.
These findings provide decision-support evidence for prioritizing deep envelope
retrofits over electrification-only pathways in temperate climates.

**Elsevier Energy Abstract Checklist:**
- [ ] Research gap stated (operational vs. embodied carbon interaction)
- [ ] Sample size / scope quantified (120 buildings, 5 archetypes, 3 climate zones)
- [ ] Software and version stated (EnergyPlus v23.1)
- [ ] Calibration validated against measured data with metric and standard (ASHRAE 14)
- [ ] LCA database and version (ecoinvent v3.10), impact method (ReCiPe 2016), and GWP source (IPCC AR6) stated
- [ ] Quantitative results with uncertainty (58 ± 6%)
- [ ] Economic metric included (LCCS, £/tCO₂e)
- [ ] Practical conclusion stated
- [ ] 200–250 words
- [ ] 4–8 keywords not repeating title words

## Example Introduction Opening (First Two Paragraphs)

Buildings account for approximately 37% of global final energy consumption and
27% of energy-related CO₂ emissions, with the majority attributed to space heating,
cooling, and domestic hot water in the residential sector [1,2].
The European Union's Renovation Wave strategy and the UK's Heat and Buildings Strategy
mandate near-zero energy standards for retrofitted dwellings by 2030, creating
an urgent need for evidence-based guidance on the most effective deep retrofit
pathways [3,4].
While operational energy reduction through improved thermal envelopes and heat pump
electrification has been extensively studied [5–8], the whole-life carbon implications
of the associated embodied carbon from retrofit materials and systems remain
insufficiently quantified at the building stock level [9].

Life-cycle assessment (LCA) offers a systematic framework for evaluating the
environmental trade-offs between operational and embodied carbon across the full
building life cycle [10].
Previous LCA studies have examined individual retrofit measures — external wall
insulation [11], triple-glazing [12], mechanical ventilation with heat recovery
(MVHR) [13] — but few have integrated LCA with calibrated building energy simulation
(BES) across statistically representative building archetypes and multiple climate
contexts [14,15].
This limitation is consequential because the optimal retrofit sequence and the
significance of embodied carbon vary substantially with climate, construction era,
and energy grid decarbonization trajectory [16].
