# Scientific Critical Evaluation and Peer Review

## Overview

Peer review is a systematic process for evaluating scientific manuscripts. Assess methodology, statistics, design, reproducibility, ethics, and reporting standards. Apply this skill for manuscript and grant review across disciplines with constructive, rigorous evaluation.

## When to Use This Skill

This skill should be used when:
- Conducting peer review of scientific manuscripts for journals
- Evaluating grant proposals and research applications
- Assessing methodology and experimental design rigor
- Reviewing statistical analyses and reporting standards
- Evaluating reproducibility and data availability
- Checking compliance with reporting guidelines (CONSORT, STROBE, PRISMA)
- Providing constructive feedback on scientific writing

**Related Resource:** The **venue-templates** skill provides `reviewer_expectations.md` with detailed guidance on what reviewers look for at different venues (Nature/Science, Cell Press, medical journals, ML conferences). Use this to calibrate your review standards to the target venue.

## Peer Review Workflow

Conduct peer review systematically through the following stages, adapting depth and focus based on the manuscript type and discipline.

### Stage 1: Initial Assessment

Begin with a high-level evaluation to determine the manuscript's scope, novelty, and overall quality.

**Key Questions:**
- What is the central research question or hypothesis?
- What are the main findings and conclusions?
- Is the work scientifically sound and significant?
- Is the work appropriate for the intended venue?
- Are there any immediate major flaws that would preclude publication?

**Output:** Brief summary (2-3 sentences) capturing the manuscript's essence and initial impression.

### Stage 2: Detailed Section-by-Section Review

Conduct a thorough evaluation of each manuscript section, documenting specific concerns and strengths.

#### Abstract and Title
- **Accuracy:** Does the abstract accurately reflect the study's content and conclusions?
- **Clarity:** Is the title specific, accurate, and informative?
- **Completeness:** Are key findings and methods summarized appropriately?
- **Accessibility:** Is the abstract comprehensible to a broad scientific audience?

#### Introduction
- **Context:** Is the background information adequate and current?
- **Rationale:** Is the research question clearly motivated and justified?
- **Novelty:** Is the work's originality and significance clearly articulated?
- **Literature:** Are relevant prior studies appropriately cited?
- **Objectives:** Are research aims/hypotheses clearly stated?

#### Methods
- **Reproducibility:** Can another researcher replicate the study from the description provided?
- **Rigor:** Are the methods appropriate for addressing the research questions?
- **Detail:** Are protocols, reagents, equipment, and parameters sufficiently described?
- **Ethics:** Are ethical approvals, consent, and data handling properly documented?
- **Statistics:** Are statistical methods appropriate, clearly described, and justified?
- **Validation:** Are controls, replicates, and validation approaches adequate?

**Critical elements to verify:**
- Sample sizes and power calculations
- Randomization and blinding procedures
- Inclusion/exclusion criteria
- Data collection protocols
- Computational methods and software versions
- Statistical tests and correction for multiple comparisons

#### Results
- **Presentation:** Are results presented logically and clearly?
- **Figures/Tables:** Are visualizations appropriate, clear, and properly labeled?
- **Statistics:** Are statistical results properly reported (effect sizes, confidence intervals, p-values)?
- **Objectivity:** Are results presented without over-interpretation?
- **Completeness:** Are all relevant results included, including negative results?
- **Reproducibility:** Are raw data or summary statistics provided?

**Common issues to identify:**
- Selective reporting of results
- Inappropriate statistical tests
- Missing error bars or measures of variability
- Over-fitting or circular analysis
- Batch effects or confounding variables
- Missing controls or validation experiments

#### Discussion
- **Interpretation:** Are conclusions supported by the data?
- **Limitations:** Are study limitations acknowledged and discussed?
- **Context:** Are findings placed appropriately within existing literature?
- **Speculation:** Is speculation clearly distinguished from data-supported conclusions?
- **Significance:** Are implications and importance clearly articulated?
- **Future directions:** Are next steps or unanswered questions discussed?

**Red flags:**
- Overstated conclusions
- Ignoring contradictory evidence
- Causal claims from correlational data
- Inadequate discussion of limitations
- Mechanistic claims without mechanistic evidence

#### References
- **Completeness:** Are key relevant papers cited?
- **Currency:** Are recent important studies included?
- **Balance:** Are contrary viewpoints appropriately cited?
- **Accuracy:** Are citations accurate and appropriate?
- **Self-citation:** Is there excessive or inappropriate self-citation?

### Stage 3: Methodological and Statistical Rigor

Evaluate the technical quality and rigor of the research with particular attention to common pitfalls.

**Statistical Assessment:**
- Are statistical assumptions met (normality, independence, homoscedasticity)?
- Are effect sizes reported alongside p-values?
- Is multiple testing correction applied appropriately?
- Are confidence intervals provided?
- Is sample size justified with power analysis?
- Are parametric vs. non-parametric tests chosen appropriately?
- Are missing data handled properly?
- Are exploratory vs. confirmatory analyses distinguished?

**Experimental Design:**
- Are controls appropriate and adequate?
- Is replication sufficient (biological and technical)?
- Are potential confounders identified and controlled?
- Is randomization properly implemented?
- Are blinding procedures adequate?
- Is the experimental design optimal for the research question?

**Computational/Bioinformatics:**
- Are computational methods clearly described and justified?
- Are software versions and parameters documented?
- Is code made available for reproducibility?
- Are algorithms and models validated appropriately?
- Are assumptions of computational methods met?
- Is batch correction applied appropriately?

**Evidence Quality Frameworks — Discipline-Specific Guidance:**

Note: Frameworks such as GRADE (Grading of Recommendations, Assessment, Development and Evaluations) and Cochrane Risk of Bias (ROB) are designed for biomedical and clinical research evidence synthesis. They are not directly applicable to computer science, engineering, or other non-clinical disciplines. When reviewing CS/AI papers, use the following alternatives instead:

- **CS/AI**: NeurIPS Reproducibility Checklist, ML Reproducibility Scorecard, ACM Artifact Review and Badging (computational artifact evaluation)
- **Engineering**: ASCE/ASME experimental validation standards, mesh convergence guidelines (for FEA/CFD)
- **General quantitative research**: Consider CONSORT (for RCTs), STROBE (for observational studies), or PRISMA (for systematic reviews) only when the study design aligns with these frameworks

When a CS/AI paper uses clinical evaluation methods (e.g., user studies with human participants), CONSORT or STROBE guidelines may become relevant for that component.

### Stage 4: Reproducibility and Transparency

Assess whether the research meets modern standards for reproducibility and open science.

**Data Availability:**
- Are raw data deposited in appropriate repositories?
- Are accession numbers provided for public databases?
- Are data sharing restrictions justified (e.g., patient privacy)?
- Are data formats standard and accessible?

**Code and Materials:**
- Is analysis code made available (GitHub, Zenodo, etc.)?
- Are unique materials available or described sufficiently for recreation?
- Are protocols detailed in sufficient depth?

**Reporting Standards:**
- Does the manuscript follow discipline-specific reporting guidelines (CONSORT, PRISMA, ARRIVE, MIAME, MINSEQE, etc.)?
- See `references/reporting_standards.md` for common guidelines
- Are all elements of the appropriate checklist addressed?

### Stage 5: Figure and Data Presentation

Evaluate the quality, clarity, and integrity of data visualization.

**Quality Checks:**
- Are figures high resolution and clearly labeled?
- Are axes properly labeled with units?
- Are error bars defined (SD, SEM, CI)?
- Are statistical significance indicators explained?
- Are color schemes appropriate and accessible (colorblind-friendly)?
- Are scale bars included for images?
- Is data visualization appropriate for the data type?

**Integrity Checks:**
- Are there signs of image manipulation (duplications, splicing)?
- Are Western blots and gels appropriately presented?
- Are representative images truly representative?
- Are all conditions shown (no selective presentation)?

**Clarity:**
- Can figures stand alone with their legends?
- Is the message of each figure immediately clear?
- Are there redundant figures or panels?
- Would data be better presented as tables or figures?

### Stage 6: Ethical Considerations

Verify that the research meets ethical standards and guidelines.

**Human Subjects:**
- Is IRB/ethics approval documented?
- Is informed consent described?
- Are vulnerable populations appropriately protected?
- Is patient privacy adequately protected?
- Are potential conflicts of interest disclosed?

**Animal Research:**
- Is IACUC or equivalent approval documented?
- Are procedures humane and justified?
- Are the 3Rs (replacement, reduction, refinement) considered?
- Are euthanasia methods appropriate?

**Research Integrity:**
- Are there concerns about data fabrication or falsification?
- Is authorship appropriate and justified?
- Are competing interests disclosed?
- Is funding source disclosed?
- Are there concerns about plagiarism or duplicate publication?

### Stage 7: Writing Quality and Clarity

Assess the manuscript's clarity, organization, and accessibility.

**Structure and Organization:**
- Is the manuscript logically organized?
- Do sections flow coherently?
- Are transitions between ideas clear?
- Is the narrative compelling and clear?

**Writing Quality:**
- Is the language clear, precise, and concise?
- Are jargon and acronyms minimized and defined?
- Is grammar and spelling correct?
- Are sentences unnecessarily complex?
- Is the passive voice overused?

**Accessibility:**
- Can a non-specialist understand the main findings?
- Are technical terms explained?
- Is the significance clear to a broad audience?

## Structuring Peer Review Reports

Organize feedback in a hierarchical structure that prioritizes issues and provides actionable guidance.

### Summary Statement

Provide a concise overall assessment (1-2 paragraphs):
- Brief synopsis of the research
- Overall recommendation (accept, minor revisions, major revisions, reject)
- Key strengths (2-3 bullet points)
- Key weaknesses (2-3 bullet points)
- Bottom-line assessment of significance and soundness

### Major Comments

List critical issues that significantly impact the manuscript's validity, interpretability, or significance. Number these sequentially for easy reference.

**Major comments typically include:**
- Fundamental methodological flaws
- Inappropriate statistical analyses
- Unsupported or overstated conclusions
- Missing critical controls or experiments
- Serious reproducibility concerns
- Major gaps in literature coverage
- Ethical concerns

**For each major comment:**
1. Clearly state the issue
2. Explain why it's problematic
3. Suggest specific solutions or additional experiments
4. Indicate if addressing it is essential for publication

### Minor Comments

List less critical issues that would improve clarity, completeness, or presentation. Number these sequentially.

**Minor comments typically include:**
- Unclear figure labels or legends
- Missing methodological details
- Typographical or grammatical errors
- Suggestions for improved data presentation
- Minor statistical reporting issues
- Supplementary analyses that would strengthen conclusions
- Requests for clarification

**For each minor comment:**
1. Identify the specific location (section, paragraph, figure)
2. State the issue clearly
3. Suggest how to address it

### Specific Line-by-Line Comments (Optional)

For manuscripts requiring detailed feedback, provide section-specific or line-by-line comments:
- Reference specific page/line numbers or sections
- Note factual errors, unclear statements, or missing citations
- Suggest specific edits for clarity

### Questions for Authors

List specific questions that need clarification:
- Methodological details that are unclear
- Seemingly contradictory results
- Missing information needed to evaluate the work
- Requests for additional data or analyses

## Tone and Approach

Maintain a constructive, professional, and collegial tone throughout the review.

**Best Practices:**
- **Be constructive:** Frame criticism as opportunities for improvement
- **Be specific:** Provide concrete examples and actionable suggestions
- **Be balanced:** Acknowledge strengths as well as weaknesses
- **Be respectful:** Remember that authors have invested significant effort
- **Be objective:** Focus on the science, not the scientists
- **Be thorough:** Don't overlook issues, but prioritize appropriately
- **Be clear:** Avoid ambiguous or vague criticism

**Avoid:**
- Personal attacks or dismissive language
- Sarcasm or condescension
- Vague criticism without specific examples
- Requesting unnecessary experiments beyond the scope
- Demanding adherence to personal preferences vs. best practices
- Revealing your identity if reviewing is double-blind

## Special Considerations by Manuscript Type

### Original Research Articles
- Emphasize rigor, reproducibility, and novelty
- Assess significance and impact
- Verify that conclusions are data-driven
- Check for complete methods and appropriate controls

### Reviews and Meta-Analyses
- Evaluate comprehensiveness of literature coverage
- Assess search strategy and inclusion/exclusion criteria
- Verify systematic approach and lack of bias
- Check for critical analysis vs. mere summarization
- For meta-analyses, evaluate statistical approach and heterogeneity

### Methods Papers
- Emphasize validation and comparison to existing methods
- Assess reproducibility and availability of protocols/code
- Evaluate improvements over existing approaches
- Check for sufficient detail for implementation

### Short Reports/Letters
- Adapt expectations for brevity
- Ensure core findings are still rigorous and significant
- Verify that format is appropriate for findings

### Preprints
- Recognize that these have not undergone formal peer review
- May be less polished than journal submissions
- Still apply rigorous standards for scientific validity
- Consider providing constructive feedback to help authors improve before journal submission

### Engineering Research Papers

**Focus Areas:**
- **Experimental Detail**: Verify that specimen dimensions, material properties, loading protocols, and failure modes are fully described with reference to ASTM/ISO/EN standards
- **Code/Standard Comparison**: Results should be compared with predictions from relevant design codes (ACI 318, AISC 360, Eurocode, AASHTO)
- **Practical Implications**: Assess whether findings are applicable to real-world engineering practice, not just academic novelty
- **Safety Factors**: Check that design recommendations include appropriate resistance/safety factors per applicable codes
- **Scale Effects**: Verify authors discuss applicability of lab-scale results to field-scale structures
- **Validation**: Numerical models (FEA, CFD) must be validated against experimental data or analytical solutions
- **Material Characterization**: All materials must be characterized using standard test methods (ASTM, EN)
- **Repeatability**: Experiments must be replicated with statistical analysis; single-test results require justification
- **Chinese Standards Compliance (GB/JGJ):** Verify that Chinese-language engineering papers reference the correct GB/JGJ standard editions. Key checks:
  - Concrete design: GB 50010 (latest 2024 revision, not the outdated 2002 version)
  - Seismic design: GB 50011 with correct fortification intensity and seismic grade
  - Steel structures: GB 50017 for design, JGJ 99 for high-rise steel
  - Load combinations: GB 50009 dead/live/wind/snow loads with correct partial safety factors (γ_G=1.3, γ_Q=1.5 for ULS)
  - Test methods: GB/T 50081 for concrete, GB/T 228.1 for metals, GB/T 50152 for structural testing
  - Material properties: Verify characteristic values match GB specifications (e.g., C30 concrete f_ck=20.1 MPa, HRB400 rebar f_yk=400 MPa)
  - Cross-reference: When authors claim compliance with both Chinese and international standards, verify consistency between GB and ACI/Eurocode provisions

**Common Issues to Watch For (see references/common_issues.md Issues 23-30):**
- Insufficient material characterization (only nominal values, no test data)
- Missing comparison with design code predictions
- Boundary conditions not matching real-world conditions
- No mesh convergence study for numerical models
- Scale effects not addressed (lab vs. field)

### Study Type Specific Checklists (Engineering)

#### Simulation/Computational Study Checklist
When reviewing numerical/computational studies (FEM, CFD, DEM, etc.):
- [ ] **Software & Version**: Commercial software (ABAQUS, ANSYS, OpenSees, etc.) version specified
- [ ] **Element Type & Mesh**: Element formulation described, mesh convergence study performed (at least 3 mesh densities)
- [ ] **Material Model**: Constitutive model justified, parameters sourced from experiments or literature with citations
- [ ] **Boundary Conditions**: BCs clearly described with figures, match real-world conditions
- [ ] **Loading Protocol**: Loading scheme matches design code or experimental protocol
- [ ] **Validation**: Model validated against at least one experimental result or analytical solution
- [ ] **Sensitivity Analysis**: Key parameters varied to assess robustness
- [ ] **Convergence Criteria**: Iteration/convergence criteria stated (force tolerance, displacement tolerance)
- [ ] **Damping/Nonlinearity**: Rayleigh damping parameters, geometric/material nonlinearity treatment
- [ ] **Comparison with Design Code**: Numerical results compared with code predictions (GB 50010, ACI 318, etc.)

#### Experimental/Laboratory Study Checklist
When reviewing physical experimental studies:
- [ ] **Specimen Design**: Dimensions, reinforcement details, material properties with standard test citations
- [ ] **Material Testing**: Concrete cylinder tests (GB/T 50081 or ASTM C39), rebar tensile tests (GB/T 228.1 or ASTM A370) with stress-strain curves
- [ ] **Test Setup**: Loading configuration, support conditions, instrumentation layout with photos/schematics
- [ ] **Loading Protocol**: Monotonic vs. cyclic, displacement-controlled vs. force-controlled, loading rate
- [ ] **Instrumentation**: Sensor types (LVDT, strain gauge, DIC), measurement range, accuracy
- [ ] **Data Acquisition**: Sampling rate, data filtering/smoothing methods
- [ ] **Failure Mode**: Clear description and photos of failure mode, comparison with predicted failure mode
- [ ] **Repeatability**: Number of replicate specimens, statistical summary (mean, std, COV)
- [ ] **Scale Effects**: Discussion of lab-to-field scale applicability
- [ ] **Standard Compliance**: Test procedures follow GB/T 50081, GB/T 50152, ASTM, or EN standards

#### Combined Simulation + Experimental Study Checklist
When reviewing studies that combine both numerical and experimental work:
- [ ] **Consistency**: Numerical model parameters match experimental material properties
- [ ] **Boundary Condition Match**: Numerical BCs replicate experimental setup (not idealized)
- [ ] **Loading Match**: Numerical loading protocol matches experimental protocol exactly
- [ ] **Quantitative Comparison**: Error metrics reported (peak load, stiffness, displacement at failure)
- [ ] **Qualitative Comparison**: Failure mode comparison with photos/contour plots
- [ ] **Parametric Study**: Numerical model extends beyond experimental range (different parameters, scales)
- [ ] **Model Limitations**: Honest discussion of where model deviates from experiment
- [ ] **Calibration Process**: If model was calibrated to experiment, calibration procedure documented
- [ ] **Prediction vs. Calibration**: Distinguish between calibrated parameters and predictive parameters

#### Structural Testing Checklist
When reviewing structural testing studies (beams, columns, connections, frames, walls):
- [ ] **Test Setup Documentation**: Test setup clearly documented with photographs and/or schematics showing support conditions, loading arrangement, and specimen dimensions
- [ ] **Loading Protocol**: Loading protocol described in detail — monotonic, cyclic (with displacement history), quasi-static, or dynamic (shake table/hybrid); include loading rate and control mode (displacement-controlled vs. force-controlled)
- [ ] **Instrumentation Plan**: Instrumentation plan documented with sensor types (LVDTs, strain gauges, accelerometers, DIC), measurement ranges, accuracy, and physical locations on specimen
- [ ] **Material Properties**: Material properties reported from standard tests (ASTM E8/E8M for steel, ASTM C39 for concrete, ASTM D695 for plastics) with stress-strain curves and characteristic values
- [ ] **Failure Mode Identification**: Failure mode clearly identified and classified (e.g., flexural, shear, bond-slip, connection failure) with photographs and comparison to predicted failure mode
- [ ] **Load-Displacement Curves**: Load-displacement (or moment-rotation) curves reported with key points marked (cracking, yielding, peak, ultimate, failure)
- [ ] **Ductility Ratios**: Ductility ratios (displacement ductility, curvature ductility) calculated and reported with definition used (e.g., yield displacement by equal energy method)
- [ ] **Energy Dissipation**: Energy dissipation capacity quantified (cumulative energy, equivalent viscous damping ratio) for cyclic tests; report energy dissipation per cycle vs. drift level

#### Finite Element Analysis Checklist
When reviewing studies involving finite element analysis (FEA) of structural/engineering systems:
- [ ] **Element Type and Mesh Convergence**: Element type (beam, shell, solid, plate) and formulation documented; mesh convergence study reported with at least 3 mesh densities showing <2% change in key output
- [ ] **Material Constitutive Models**: Material constitutive models described with full parameter set (e.g., concrete: compressive strength, tensile strength, modulus, plasticity parameters; steel: yield strength, hardening modulus, kinematic vs. isotropic hardening); parameters sourced from experiments or literature with citations
- [ ] **Boundary Conditions**: Boundary conditions justified and documented with figures; connection idealizations (pinned, fixed, semi-rigid) match physical behavior
- [ ] **Validation Against Experiment**: FEA results validated against experimental results (if available) with quantitative error metrics (peak load error, displacement error, stiffness error, RMSE)
- [ ] **Sensitivity Analysis**: Sensitivity analysis on key modeling assumptions (mesh density, material model, boundary conditions, loading protocol) reported
- [ ] **Software and Solver Settings**: Software name, version, and solver settings reported (e.g., ABAQUS 2024, static Riks method, Newton-Raphson convergence criteria, arc-length increment)

### Computer Science Papers

**Focus Areas:**
- **Reproducibility**: Verify code availability, random seeds, hardware specifications, and software versions are reported
- **Baselines**: Check that comparisons use strong, recent baselines with equal computational resources
- **Ablation Studies**: Each claimed contribution should be isolated and tested independently
- **Novelty Assessment**: Distinguish between genuine advances and incremental improvements; assess whether the contribution is sufficient
- **Benchmark Rigor**: Verify that benchmarks are standard, results are not cherry-picked, and multiple datasets are used
- **Statistical Rigor**: Multiple runs with confidence intervals and significance tests; single-run results are insufficient
- **Compute Cost**: Training time, GPU hours, FLOPs, and inference latency should be reported
- **Dataset Ethics**: Check for bias analysis, fairness considerations, and proper licensing

**Common Issues to Watch For (see references/common_issues.md Issues 31-38):**
- Weak or outdated baselines that make methods look better
- No ablation study isolating each contribution
- Dataset leakage between train/test splits
- Cherry-picked results or selected examples
- Missing hyperparameter search details
- Non-reproducible results due to missing seeds/code
- Overclaiming on synthetic/toy benchmarks

**Special Considerations by CS Subfield:**
- **ML/AI**: Training curves, convergence behavior, generalization to out-of-distribution data
- **Systems**: Throughput, latency, scalability, failure modes, recovery procedures
- **Theory**: Correctness proofs, complexity bounds, comparison with known lower bounds
- **HCI**: User study design, effect sizes, ecological validity, ethical considerations
- **Security**: Threat model clarity, responsible disclosure, real-world attack feasibility

#### Computer Science / AI Manuscript Checklist

**Research Quality:**
- [ ] Research question clearly stated and well-motivated
- [ ] Novelty clearly articulated relative to prior work
- [ ] Claims supported by sufficient evidence
- [ ] Limitations honestly discussed

**Methodology:**
- [ ] Algorithm/method clearly described with sufficient detail for reproduction
- [ ] Complexity analysis provided (time, space, communication)
- [ ] Pseudocode or implementation details sufficient for reimplementation
- [ ] Design choices justified (architecture, loss function, optimizer, etc.)
- [ ] Baselines are fair and include SOTA methods
- [ ] Datasets appropriate, standard, and properly described

**Experimental Evaluation:**
- [ ] Ablation study isolates contribution of each component
- [ ] Multiple random seeds (≥3) with mean ± std reported
- [ ] Statistical significance tested where claims depend on numerical differences
- [ ] Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis included
- [ ] Training curves (loss, metric vs. epoch) reported
- [ ] Compute budget reported (GPU hours, parameters, FLOPs)
- [ ] OOD / robustness evaluation included
- [ ] Fairness / bias analysis included (when model affects people)

**Reproducibility:**
- [ ] Code available or will be available at publication
- [ ] Random seeds and exact configurations reported
- [ ] Software versions and dependencies specified
- [ ] Data splits and preprocessing fully documented
- [ ] Hardware specification reported
- [ ] NeurIPS/ICML reproducibility checklist completed

**Reporting:**
- [ ] Results presented with proper statistical treatment (not just best-run numbers)
- [ ] Tables/figures clearly labeled with error bars or confidence intervals
- [ ] Comparison methods are properly cited and implemented
- [ ] Negative results and failure cases discussed
- [ ] Broader impact / societal implications discussed (if applicable)

**LLM-Specific (if applicable):**
- [ ] Training data details (size, source, cutoff date) reported
- [ ] Data contamination check performed and reported
- [ ] Evaluation on decontaminated subsets when using public benchmarks
- [ ] Prompt templates and inference parameters documented
- [ ] Human evaluation protocol described (if applicable)
- [ ] Responsible AI considerations addressed (bias, safety, misuse potential)

### AI for Engineering Papers — Integrated Review Checklist

**When to use this checklist:** The paper uses ML/DL (or other AI methods) to solve a civil, structural, mechanical, or environmental engineering problem — for example structural health monitoring, crack detection, energy demand prediction, seismic response estimation, fatigue life prediction, or HVAC control optimization. These papers sit at the intersection of CS and engineering and require evaluation from both perspectives simultaneously.

**Dual-lens review philosophy:** A paper that is only CS-rigorous (good ablation, strong baselines, proper cross-validation) but ignores engineering validity (physically impossible predictions, unrealistic boundary conditions, no code compliance) is not acceptable. Conversely, a paper with solid engineering grounding but weak ML methodology (no ablation, single run, weak baselines) is equally deficient. Both lenses must pass.

**Integrated Review Checklist:**

1. **Engineering Problem Validity**
   - Is the engineering problem practically relevant and clearly motivated?
   - Are the engineering constraints (loads, materials, environmental conditions) realistic and representative of field conditions?
   - Is the problem scope appropriate — not trivially solvable by existing engineering methods, and not so broad that the ML approach is unfocused?

2. **Data Quality and Engineering Provenance**
   - Is training data sourced from real engineering measurements (field sensors, laboratory tests, operational records)?
   - If synthetic/simulated data is used, is it validated against real measurements and are its limitations acknowledged?
   - Are boundary conditions, loading regimes, and environmental conditions documented for each data sample?
   - Is the data collection protocol described (sensor types, sampling rates, measurement uncertainty)?
   - Are data limitations (gaps, sensor failures, outliers) reported and their impact discussed?

3. **Model Choice Justification**
   - Why is ML/DL necessary? Is the problem too complex for empirical equations, code-based methods, or FEM?
   - Is the chosen architecture justified over simpler alternatives (linear regression, decision trees, random forests)?
   - Is model complexity proportionate to problem complexity?
   - Are physics-informed approaches (PINNs, physics-informed loss terms, hard constraint layers) considered where appropriate?

4. **Baseline Comparison — Dual Baselines Required**
   - **ML baselines:** At least 2-3 ML/DL baselines (e.g., CNN vs. LSTM vs. Transformer, or RF vs. XGBoost vs. MLP)
   - **Engineering baselines:** At least 1-2 traditional engineering baselines (code-based predictions, empirical equations, FEM results, hand calculations)
   - Are baselines given equal hyperparameter tuning budget?
   - Is the comparison fair (same data, same train/test split, same evaluation metrics)?

5. **Physical Consistency**
   - Do predictions violate known physical laws? (conservation of energy/mass/momentum, equilibrium, monotonicity where expected)
   - Are predictions dimensionally consistent?
   - Are physically impossible outputs (negative stiffness, tensile capacity exceeding material strength) detected and handled?
   - If physics violations occur, are they documented and explained?
   - Are physics-informed constraints or post-processing filters used to enforce physical plausibility?

6. **Generalization Beyond Random Splits**
   - Is generalization tested with domain-relevant splits, not just random train/test partitioning?
     - **Temporal split:** Train on older data, test on newer data (simulates deployment)
     - **Spatial split:** Train on some structures/locations, test on unseen ones
     - **Structure-wise split:** Train on some structural typologies, test on others
     - **Material-wise split:** Train on some material grades, test on others
     - **Condition-wise split:** Train under some loading/environmental conditions, test under others
   - Is out-of-distribution (OOD) performance reported and discussed?
   - Are failure modes on OOD samples analyzed?

7. **Uncertainty Quantification**
   - Are prediction intervals reported, not just point estimates?
   - Is uncertainty quantified using appropriate methods (ensemble methods, MC dropout, conformal prediction, Bayesian neural networks)?
   - Are prediction intervals calibrated (do 90% prediction intervals contain ~90% of observations)?
   - Is epistemic uncertainty (model uncertainty) distinguished from aleatoric uncertainty (data noise)?
   - Are high-uncertainty predictions flagged for human review in a deployment scenario?

8. **Deployment Feasibility**
   - Is inference time reported and compared with traditional methods?
   - Are hardware requirements specified (GPU vs. CPU, memory, edge device compatibility)?
   - Is the model size (parameters, file size) reported?
   - Are edge/embedded deployment considerations addressed (model compression, quantization, pruning)?
   - Is the total pipeline cost (data collection + preprocessing + inference) compared with traditional engineering analysis cost?

9. **Engineering Interpretability**
   - Can engineers understand WHY the model makes specific predictions?
   - Are interpretability methods applied (SHAP, LIME, attention maps, gradient-based attribution, layer-wise relevance propagation)?
   - Do feature importances align with domain knowledge (e.g., concrete strength is important for crack prediction)?
   - Are attention maps or activation visualizations physically meaningful?
   - Can the model's reasoning be communicated to practicing engineers who will use it?

10. **Code and Regulation Compliance**
    - Do model outputs meet applicable design code safety factors and acceptance criteria (ASCE, ACI, Eurocode, AISC, GB)?
    - If the model is used for design decisions, is the reliability index (beta) or probability of failure reported?
    - Are code-required partial safety factors applied to model predictions before use in design?
    - Is the model's failure mode (false negative = unsafe prediction) more costly than its success mode?
    - Are regulatory acceptance criteria for AI-assisted decision-making in engineering met?

**Scoring Guidance:**
- A paper should pass at least 8 of 10 criteria for acceptance at a top venue.
- Criteria 1 (problem validity), 4 (dual baselines), 5 (physical consistency), and 6 (domain-relevant generalization) are critical — failure on any of these is typically a major revision or rejection.
- Criteria 7 (UQ) and 10 (code compliance) are essential for papers proposing models for design or safety-critical applications.

### Environmental Engineering (Carbon/Energy/HVAC) Papers

**Focus Areas:**
- **Carbon/LCA Completeness**: For life cycle assessments, verify goal/scope, system boundary, functional unit, LCIA method, and sensitivity analysis per ISO 14040/14044
- **GHG Protocol Compliance**: Verify scope delineation (1/2/3), emission factor sources, and GWP time horizon
- **Building Energy Simulation**: Verify simulation software, weather data, envelope specs, HVAC system details, and calibration methodology
- **HVAC System Evaluation**: Check that both energy performance and thermal comfort (ASHRAE 55) are assessed
- **Standards Compliance**: Verify alignment with ASHRAE standards (55, 62.1, 90.1, 209) and ISO 14064
- **Practical Applicability**: Assess whether findings are applicable to real buildings/systems, not just theoretical models
- **Economic Viability**: Check for life cycle cost analysis, payback period, or NPV when proposing new systems

**Common Issues to Watch For:**
- Incomplete LCA (missing stages, undefined boundaries, no sensitivity analysis)
- Building simulation without calibration against measured data
- Missing HVAC system specifications or baseline definition
- Carbon accounting without clear scope delineation
- Comfort/IAQ claims without reference to ASHRAE standards

#### Environmental Engineering (Carbon/Energy/HVAC) Papers Checklist

**Carbon and LCA:**
- [ ] System boundaries clearly defined (cradle-to-gate, cradle-to-grave, etc.)
- [ ] Functional unit appropriate and clearly stated
- [ ] Data sources for life cycle inventory documented
- [ ] Allocation methods justified
- [ ] Sensitivity analysis on key assumptions
- [ ] Results compared with literature values
- [ ] Carbon emission factors and their sources documented

**Building Energy:**
- [ ] Building model calibrated against measured data (if available)
- [ ] Weather data source and typicality discussed
- [ ] HVAC system specifications complete
- [ ] Simulation software and version reported
- [ ] Key assumptions (occupancy, schedules, setpoints) documented
- [ ] Results presented as EUI with appropriate normalization
- [ ] Uncertainty/sensitivity analysis included

**HVAC Systems:**
- [ ] Baseline system clearly defined (ASHRAE 90.1 or equivalent)
- [ ] Performance metrics include both energy and comfort
- [ ] Economic analysis included (payback, LCC)
- [ ] Scalability and practical deployment discussed
- [ ] Control strategies described in sufficient detail
- [ ] Comparison with existing solutions in literature

**Standards Compliance:**
- [ ] Relevant ASHRAE standards cited and followed
- [ ] ISO standards for LCA/carbon followed where applicable
- [ ] Local building energy codes referenced
- [ ] Climate zone and location clearly specified

### CS+Biomedical Paper Review Checklist

**When to use this checklist:** The paper applies machine learning, deep learning, NLP, or other computational methods to biomedical problems — medical imaging analysis, drug discovery, genomics/bioinformatics, clinical NLP, or clinical decision support. These papers require evaluation from both computational rigor and clinical relevance perspectives.

**Dual-lens review philosophy:** A CS+Biomedical paper must be both computationally sound (proper ML methodology, ablation, baselines, reproducibility) and clinically meaningful (relevant question, validated data, external validation, clinical utility). Failure on either lens warrants major revision or rejection.

**Integrated Review Checklist:**

1. **Clinical Relevance**
   - Is the clinical question well-defined and significant?
   - Is the target patient population clearly specified (inclusion/exclusion criteria, disease stage, comorbidities)?
   - Is the clinical need articulated — why existing approaches are insufficient?
   - Would clinicians actually use this system? Is the workflow integration realistic?
   - Is the clinical outcome meaningful (not just a surrogate or convenience metric)?

2. **Data Quality and Annotation**
   - Is the dataset properly described (source, size, demographics, collection period)?
   - Are labels validated by domain experts (board-certified radiologists, pathologists, clinicians)?
   - Is inter-annotator agreement reported (Cohen's kappa, Fleiss' kappa) and acceptable (kappa > 0.6)?
   - Is the annotation protocol documented in sufficient detail for replication?
   - Are data quality issues addressed (missing data, class imbalance, label noise)?
   - Is patient-level splitting used to prevent data leakage (not image-level or record-level)?

3. **Bias Assessment**
   - **Selection bias**: Is the dataset representative of the target clinical population? Are referral patterns, geographic distribution, and demographic composition reported?
   - **Label bias**: Are labels derived from a gold standard (histopathology, expert consensus) or a surrogate (ICD codes, radiology reports)? If surrogate, are limitations acknowledged?
   - **Measurement bias**: Are there systematic differences in data acquisition (scanner manufacturers, protocols, institutions) that could confound results?
   - **Temporal bias**: Is the training data from a different era than the test data? Are temporal shifts in clinical practice addressed?
   - **Demographic fairness**: Is model performance stratified by age, sex, race/ethnicity, and socioeconomic status? Are disparities reported?

4. **External Validation**
   - Is the model tested on an external dataset from a different institution, geographic region, or time period?
   - Is the external validation dataset adequately described (size, demographics, acquisition protocol)?
   - Does performance degrade on external data? If so, is the drop analyzed and discussed?
   - For genomics: Is validation performed on an independent cohort (e.g., TCGA train, GEO/ICGC validation)?
   - For NLP: Is temporal or cross-institutional validation performed?

5. **Clinical Utility**
   - Does the model improve over existing clinical workflow or decision tools?
   - Is decision curve analysis (DCA) or net benefit analysis performed?
   - Is the model compared with existing clinical scoring systems (not just ML baselines)?
   - Is the incremental benefit over current practice quantified?
   - Is the clinical deployment pathway described (integration with PACS, EHR, clinical workflow)?
   - For medical imaging: Is comparison with radiologist performance reported with appropriate statistical tests (McNemar, DeLong)?

6. **Regulatory Pathway**
   - Is the intended use (diagnostic, screening, triage, monitoring) clearly defined?
   - Are FDA 510(k)/De Novo/PMA or CE marking considerations discussed?
   - Is the software as a medical device (SaMD) classification addressed?
   - Is the monitoring and update strategy for deployed models described?
   - Are risk mitigation strategies for false positives/negatives discussed?

7. **Ethical Approval and Data Governance**
   - Is IRB/Ethics committee approval documented with protocol number?
   - Is informed consent or waiver of consent described?
   - Is data de-identification methodology reported (HIPAA safe harbor, expert determination)?
   - Are data use agreements and sharing restrictions justified?
   - Is the study preregistered (for clinical trials involving AI interventions)?

8. **Reporting Guideline Compliance**
   - **TRIPOD+AI**: Is the checklist completed for prediction/prognosis models? (model development, validation, calibration, discrimination, clinical utility)
   - **CLAIM**: Is the checklist completed for medical imaging AI studies? (data annotation, model architecture, training, evaluation, interpretability)
   - **SPIRIT-AI / CONSORT-AI**: Are the checklists completed for clinical trials involving AI interventions?
   - **DECIDE-AI**: Is the checklist completed for early-stage clinical AI evaluations?
   - Are all mandatory items from the applicable guideline addressed in the manuscript?

**Scoring Guidance:**
- A paper should pass at least 6 of 8 criteria for acceptance at a top venue.
- Criteria 1 (clinical relevance), 2 (data quality), 3 (bias), and 4 (external validation) are critical — failure on any is typically a major revision or rejection.
- Criterion 8 (reporting compliance) is mandatory — missing TRIPOD+AI or CLAIM checklist is a major issue.

### Chinese Standards Compliance Review (中国规范合规审查)

When reviewing Chinese-language engineering papers or papers claiming compliance with Chinese standards:

#### Design Standards (设计规范)
- **GB 50010** (混凝土结构设计规范): Check concrete grade, rebar grade, cover thickness, load combinations
- **GB 50011** (建筑抗震设计规范): Check seismic fortification category, seismic grade, detail requirements
- **GB 50017** (钢结构设计标准): Check steel grade, connection design, stability checks
- **GB 50009** (建筑结构荷载规范): Check dead/live/wind/snow load values and combinations
- **JGJ 3** (高层建筑混凝土结构技术规程): Check drift limits, stiffness requirements for tall buildings
- **GB 55015** (建筑节能与可再生能源利用通用规范): Check energy efficiency requirements

#### Test Method Standards (试验方法标准)
- **GB/T 50081** (混凝土力学性能试验方法): Check specimen size, loading rate, curing age
- **GB/T 228.1** (金属材料拉伸试验): Check strain rate, specimen dimensions
- **GB/T 50152** (混凝土结构试验方法): Check structural test procedures
- **GB/T 50129** (砌体基本力学性能试验方法): For masonry studies

#### Common Non-Compliance Issues
- Using outdated standard editions (e.g., GB 50010-2002 instead of 2010/2024 revision)
- Incorrect load combination factors (γ_G, γ_Q, γ_W values)
- Material characteristic values not matching standard tables
- Test specimen dimensions not meeting standard requirements
- Missing standard citation for test procedures
- Mixing Chinese and international standards without reconciliation

**Common Presentation Issues to Flag:**

- Element overlaps obscuring data
- Insufficient contrast (text hard to read)
- Figures too complex or illegible

**Major Issues (Should Fix):**

- Minor alignment inconsistencies
- Could benefit from more white space

## Responding to Reviewer Comments

### Workflow for Rebuttal

When responding to peer review feedback, follow this systematic approach:

#### Step 1: Normalize and Classify
For each reviewer comment, create an atomic item with:
- **Stable ID:** R1-C1, R1-C2, R2-C1, etc.
- **Classification:** editorial, text_only, evidence_gap, experiment_gap, claim_scope, or cannot_fully_address
- **Severity:** critical (blocks acceptance), major (significantly affects score), minor (improvement)
- **Route:** text_revision, evidence_repackaging, literature_positioning, supplementary_experiment, claim_downgrade, or explicit_limitation

#### Step 2: Analyze Before Executing
Before running new experiments or making major changes:
- Determine if the issue is wording/clarity, literature/positioning, evidence presentation, or genuinely new experiments
- Check if existing evidence already addresses the concern (just needs better presentation)
- Identify whether the concern requires claim narrowing rather than more data

#### Step 3: Draft Response Strategy
For each comment, decide one of:
- **Agree and fix:** Make the change, show the improvement
- **Partially agree:** Acknowledge the valid part, explain the rest
- **Clarify:** Provide additional explanation or context
- **Respectful disagree:** Present evidence-based counter-argument

#### Step 4: Write the Response Letter

**Structure:**
1. Brief appreciation (1-2 sentences, not excessive)
2. Overall response summarizing revision strategy
3. Point-by-point responses with stable item IDs

**Style Rules:**
- Write in calm, direct, precise author voice
- Sound like authors clarifying the record, not asking for approval
- Lead with substance, not politeness
- Answer the reviewer concern directly in first 1-2 sentences
- Include revised text or data when applicable
- Do not use bullets or numbered lists inside response paragraphs
- If pushing back, lead with evidence, scope, or feasibility constraints
- Use explicit placeholders like [[AUTHOR TO FILL]] for unknown specifics

**Common Mistakes to Avoid:**
- Empty politeness without substance
- Evasive wording that doesn't address the concern
- Pretending a limitation is solved when it's only reframed
- Promising future work when current evidence should be presented
- Ignoring inconvenient concerns
- Answering rhetoric when the issue requires evidence

#### Step 5: Manuscript Revision
Track explicit text deltas:
- Section affected
- Old claim/weakness
- New wording or scope
- Evidence basis for change

### Quality Gate for Rebuttal Package
Before submission:
- [ ] Every reviewer comment has a response
- [ ] Critical and major comments are fully addressed
- [ ] Response letter is professional and evidence-based
- [ ] Manuscript changes are clearly documented
- [ ] New experiments (if any) are properly integrated
- [ ] Claim scope is adjusted if evidence warrants it
- [ ] Limitations are honestly acknowledged

## Final Checklist

Before finalizing the review, verify:

- [ ] Summary statement clearly conveys overall assessment
- [ ] Major concerns are clearly identified and justified
- [ ] Suggested revisions are specific and actionable
- [ ] Minor issues are noted but properly categorized
- [ ] Statistical methods have been evaluated
- [ ] Reproducibility and data availability assessed
- [ ] Ethical considerations verified
- [ ] Figures and tables evaluated for quality and integrity
- [ ] Writing quality assessed
- [ ] Tone is constructive and professional throughout
- [ ] Review is thorough but proportionate to manuscript scope
- [ ] Recommendation is consistent with identified issues
