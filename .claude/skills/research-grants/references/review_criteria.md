# Review Criteria by Funding Agency

## Overview

Understanding how reviewers evaluate your proposal is essential for writing a competitive application. Each agency has distinct review criteria, scoring systems, and reviewer expectations.

## NSF Review Criteria

### Two Core Criteria (Equally Weighted)

#### Criterion 1: Intellectual Merit

**What Reviewers Evaluate**:
- Importance of the proposed activity to advancing knowledge
- Qualifications of the proposer and team
- Creativity, originality, and potentially transformative concepts
- Conception and organization of the proposed activity
- Sufficient access to resources

**Scoring**: Excellent, Very Good, Good, Fair, Poor

**Tips for NSF Intellectual Merit**:
- Lead with the knowledge gap and its significance
- Demonstrate your unique qualifications
- Show preliminary data or proof-of-concept
- Present a clear, logical research plan
- Highlight transformative potential

#### Criterion 2: Broader Impacts

**What Reviewers Evaluate**:
- Benefit to society and desired societal outcomes
- Broadening participation of underrepresented groups
- Enhanced infrastructure for research and education
- Improved scientific and technological understanding
- Partnerships between academia, industry, and others

**Critical**: Broader Impacts carry EQUAL weight with Intellectual Merit. Weak broader impacts are a common reason for rejection.

**Tips for NSF Broader Impacts**:
- Address at least one of the five pillars substantively
- Be specific and measurable ("train 5 graduate students" not "train students")
- Include a timeline and assessment plan
- Integrate with the research (not an afterthought)
- Budget for broader impacts activities

### NSF Merit Review Process

1. **Panel Review**: 3-5 reviewers per proposal
2. **Advisory Panel**: Makes funding recommendations
3. **Program Officer**: Makes final funding decision
4. **Timeline**: 6-12 months from submission to decision

## NIH Review Criteria

### Five Scored Criteria (1-9 Scale)

#### 1. Significance

- Does the project address an important problem?
- If the aims are achieved, how will scientific knowledge be improved?
- How will the concepts, methods, treatments, or services be changed?

#### 2. Investigator(s)

- Are the PI and collaborators well-suited to the project?
- Do they have appropriate experience and training?
- Have they demonstrated an ongoing record of accomplishments?
- If early-stage investigators, do they have appropriate training?

#### 3. Innovation

- Does the application challenge existing paradigms?
- Are novel concepts, approaches, or methods being employed?
- Are the aims original and innovative?

#### 4. Approach

- Are the overall strategy, methodology, and analyses well-reasoned and appropriate?
- Are potential problems and alternative strategies presented?
- Has the rigor of the research been addressed?
- Has biological variables (sex/gender) been addressed?

#### 5. Environment

- Will the scientific environment contribute to the probability of success?
- Are the institutional support, equipment, and other resources adequate?

### Additional Review Considerations (Not Scored)

- Protection of human subjects
- Inclusion of women, minorities, and children
- Vertebrate animals
- Biohazards
- Resubmission response (for A1 applications)

### NIH Scoring System

| Score | Descriptor | Funding Likelihood |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| 1 | Exceptional | Very high |
| 2 | Outstanding | High |
| 3 | Excellent | Moderate-high |
| 4 | Very Good | Moderate |
| 5 | Good | Low-moderate |
| 6 | Satisfactory | Low |
| 7 | Fair | Very low |
| 8 | Marginal | Unlikely |
| 9 | Poor | Not funded |

### NIH Percentile and Payline

- **Percentile**: Your score relative to other applications reviewed by the same study section
- **Payline**: The percentile cutoff for funding (varies by institute, typically 7-12 percentile)
- **A1 strategy**: Address every criticism from A0; introduction to resubmission is critical

## DOE Review Criteria

### Varies by Office, But Generally Includes:

1. **Scientific/Technical Merit** (30-40%)
   - Quality and innovation of the research approach
   - Scientific foundation and feasibility
   - Potential for significant advances

2. **Qualifications of PI and Team** (20-25%)
   - Relevant experience and expertise
   - Track record of publications and funding
   - Team composition and management plan

3. **Relevance to Agency Mission** (20-30%)
   - Alignment with DOE priorities
   - Potential for technology transfer
   - Impact on energy, environment, or national security

4. **Budget and Cost Effectiveness** (10-15%)
   - Reasonableness of the budget
   - Cost-sharing (if required)
   - Efficient use of resources

5. **Facilities and Equipment** (5-10%)
   - Access to necessary resources
   - National laboratory partnerships

### DOE-Specific Considerations

- **TRL Advancement**: Show clear technology readiness level progression
- **National Lab Partnerships**: Strongly encouraged for many programs
- **Cost Sharing**: Required for some programs (ARPA-E, EERE)
- **Technology Transfer**: Path to deployment is important

## DARPA Review Criteria

### Five Primary Criteria

1. **Innovation and Potential Impact**
   - Is this a breakthrough capability?
   - Does it pass the "DARPA-hard" test?
   - What is the potential military and commercial impact?

2. **Technical Approach**
   - Is the technical approach sound and feasible?
   - Are risks identified and mitigated?
   - Are metrics and milestones defined?

3. **Team Qualifications**
   - Does the team have the right expertise?
   - Is the team size appropriate?
   - Can they execute on the proposed timeline?

4. **Cost and Schedule**
   - Is the budget realistic?
   - Is the schedule achievable?
   - Are resources adequate?

5. **Transition Potential**
   - How will the technology transition to DoD or commercial use?
   - Is there a clear transition partner?
   - What is the path to deployment?

### The Heilmeier Catechism

DARPA proposals should implicitly or explicitly answer:

1. What are you trying to do? (No jargon)
2. How is it done today? What are the limitations?
3. What is new and why do you think it will be successful?
4. Who cares? If you succeed, what difference does it make?
5. What are the risks?
6. How much will it cost?
7. How long will it take?
8. What are the midterm and final "exams" to check for success?

## NSFC Review Criteria (China)

### Four Evaluation Dimensions

1. **Scientific Value** (创新性)
   - Novelty and originality of the research
   - Potential to advance the field

2. **Research Foundation** (研究基础)
   - PI's publication record and expertise
   - Preliminary results

3. **Feasibility** (可行性)
   - Soundness of the research plan
   - Reasonableness of the timeline

4. **Research Conditions** (研究条件)
   - Laboratory facilities and equipment
   - Institutional support

### NSFC-Specific Features

- **Young Scientists Fund**: For researchers under 35 (men) or 40 (women)
- **General Program**: 3-year grants, ~600-800K RMB
- **Key Program**: Larger grants requiring team collaboration
- **Major Program**: Very large, multi-year strategic projects

## DOT/FHWA Review Criteria

### State Planning and Research (SPR)

1. **Relevance**: Alignment with state transportation priorities
2. **Technical Merit**: Soundness of research methodology
3. **Feasibility**: Realistic timeline and budget
4. **Implementation Potential**: Path to practice

### University Transportation Centers (UTC)

1. **Research Excellence**: Quality and innovation of proposed research
2. **Education and Workforce Development**: Student training plans
3. **Technology Transfer**: Implementation and deployment strategy
4. **Stakeholder Engagement**: Collaboration with DOTs and industry

## Self-Assessment Checklist

Use this checklist to evaluate your proposal before submission:

### NSF Self-Assessment
- [ ] Intellectual Merit clearly demonstrated
- [ ] Broader Impacts addressed substantively (at least one pillar)
- [ ] Broader Impacts are specific and measurable
- [ ] Budget includes broader impacts activities
- [ ] Data Management Plan included
- [ ] Postdoctoral Mentoring Plan included (if applicable)

### NIH Self-Assessment
- [ ] All five criteria addressed
- [ ] Rigor and reproducibility section included
- [ ] Biological variables addressed
- [ ] Key resources authenticated
- [ ] Human subjects or vertebrate animals sections complete
- [ ] Budget follows modular or detailed format correctly

### DARPA Self-Assessment
- [ ] Heilmeier Catechism questions answered
- [ ] Quantitative go/no-go metrics defined
- [ ] Risk matrix with mitigation strategies included
- [ ] Transition plan described
- [ ] Phase structure clearly delineated

### Engineering-Specific
- [ ] Materials and methods described in sufficient detail for replication
- [ ] Comparison with relevant design codes (ACI, AISC, Eurocode) included
- [ ] Safety factors and code compliance discussed
- [ ] Scale effects acknowledged
- [ ] Validation approach described

### CS-Specific
- [ ] Reproducibility plan included (code, data, environment)
- [ ] Baselines given equal tuning budget
- [ ] Ablation studies planned
- [ ] Compute cost reported
- [ ] Datasets properly described and licensed
