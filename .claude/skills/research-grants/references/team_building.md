# Team Building and Qualifications Guide

## Overview

The strength of your team is a critical factor in grant review. Reviewers evaluate whether the team has the right expertise, experience, and capacity to execute the proposed research. A well-constructed team demonstrates complementary skills, proven track records, and clear roles.

## Team Composition Principles

### 1. Complementary Expertise

- Each team member should bring unique, essential skills
- Avoid redundancy in expertise
- Cover all major methodological needs of the project
- Include both depth (specialists) and breadth (generalists)

### 2. Appropriate Size

- Match team size to project scope
- Too small: insufficient capacity
- Too large: management overhead, diffusion of responsibility
- Rule of thumb: 3-6 key personnel for a standard R01 or NSF grant

### 3. Demonstrated Collaboration

- Prior joint publications or projects strengthen the team
- Letters of collaboration should describe specific contributions
- Show how team members have worked together successfully

### 4. Institutional Diversity

- Multi-institutional teams can bring diverse resources
- Industry partnerships demonstrate real-world relevance
- International collaborations (when justified) add value

## Team Roles

### Principal Investigator (PI)

**Responsibilities**:
- Overall project leadership and management
- Scientific direction and decision-making
- Budget oversight
- Progress reporting to funding agency
- Mentoring junior team members

**What Reviewers Look For**:
- Strong publication record in the proposed area
- Prior funding history (especially for NIH)
- Leadership experience
- Time commitment adequate for the project

### Co-Principal Investigators (Co-PIs)

**Responsibilities**:
- Lead specific aims or work packages
- Contribute specialized expertise
- Co-mentor students and postdocs
- Share management responsibilities

**Selection Criteria**:
- Expertise that complements the PI
- Track record in the specific methodology
- Availability and commitment
- Institutional resources they bring

### Senior Personnel

**Types**:
- Senior researchers with specific expertise
- Consultants with specialized knowledge
- Advisory board members

**Justification**:
- Explain why their expertise is needed
- Describe their specific contribution
- Note their availability and commitment

### Postdoctoral Researchers

**Selection Criteria**:
- Recent PhD in relevant field
- Strong publication record
- Relevant technical skills
- Career goals aligned with the project

**Training Plan** (required for NSF):
- Technical skills development
- Professional development opportunities
- Mentoring structure
- Career preparation

### Graduate Students

**Selection Criteria**:
- Strong academic record
- Research interest aligned with project
- Relevant coursework and skills
- Potential for independent research

**Integration with Thesis**:
- How the project aligns with their thesis research
- Timeline for degree completion
- Co-advising arrangements (if applicable)

## Letters of Collaboration

### Required Elements

1. **Specific contribution**: What exactly will they do?
2. **Time commitment**: How much effort will they dedicate?
3. **Resources**: What institutional resources do they bring?
4. **Duration**: How long will they be involved?
5. **No overlap**: Confirm no overlap with other funding

### Template

```
Dear Dr. [PI Name],

I am writing to confirm my collaboration on your proposed project, 
"[Project Title]."

I will contribute expertise in [specific area] to [specific aim/task]. 
My contribution will include:
- [Specific deliverable 1]
- [Specific deliverable 2]

I estimate dedicating approximately [X] person-months per year to this 
collaboration. My institution, [Institution Name], will provide 
[specific resources: lab access, computing, equipment, etc.].

This collaboration is not currently supported by other funding, and 
I have no overlap with other funded projects.

Sincerely,
[Collaborator Name]
[Title, Institution]
```

### Common Mistakes

- **Too vague**: "I support this project" without specific contributions
- **No time commitment**: Not stating effort level
- **Copy-paste letters**: Identical letters for multiple collaborators
- **Missing signature**: Letters must be signed

## Biosketch Requirements by Agency

### NSF Biosketch (3 pages maximum)

**Sections**:
1. **Professional Preparation**: Education (degree, field, institution, year)
2. **Appointments**: Academic and professional positions
3. **Products**: Up to 10 most relevant publications
4. **Synergistic Activities**: Up to 5 examples

**Key Points**:
- Focus on relevance to the proposed research
- Include only publications directly related to the project
- Synergistic activities should demonstrate broader impact

### NIH Biosketch (5 pages maximum)

**Sections**:
1. **Personal Statement**: Why you are qualified (4,000 characters)
2. **Positions, Scientific Appointments, and Honors**
3. **Contributions to Science** (up to 5 contributions, 1 page each)

**Key Points**:
- Personal statement should connect your experience to the specific aims
- Each contribution should describe the problem, your role, and the impact
- Include PMCID numbers for all NIH-funded publications

### DOE CV

**Sections**:
1. Education and training
2. Relevant experience
3. Selected publications (typically 10-20)
4. Current and pending support
5. Synergistic activities

### DARPA Team Qualifications

**Focus Areas**:
- Technical expertise relevant to the program
- Experience with DARPA-style programs (if any)
- Prototype development experience
- Technology transition experience

## Engineering Team Considerations

### Industry Partners

- **Value**: Real-world relevance, testing facilities, deployment pathways
- **How to Engage**: Letters of support, in-kind contributions, advisory board
- **IP Considerations**: Define IP ownership and licensing upfront
- **Cost Sharing**: Industry partners may provide cost sharing

### National Laboratory Partnerships

- **Benefits**: Access to unique facilities, large-scale testing, expertise
- **How to Engage**: Subcontracts, collaborative research agreements
- **DOE Requirements**: Many DOE programs require or encourage lab partnerships
- **Logistics**: Lab access fees, scheduling, safety training

### Testing Facilities

- **Shake Tables**: UCSD, SUNY Buffalo, NHERI facilities
- **Wind Tunnels**: University and government facilities
- **Large-Scale Testing**: Access through NHERI, NEES
- **Computational**: XSEDE/ACCESS, national lab HPC

## CS Team Considerations

### Open-Source Community

- **Value**: Software sustainability, user base, contributions
- **How to Engage**: Existing open-source projects, GitHub organizations
- **Sustainability Plan**: How will software be maintained post-grant?

### Industry Collaboration

- **Benefits**: Real-world datasets, deployment environments, user feedback
- **Considerations**: Data sharing agreements, publication rights, IP
- **Common Arrangements**: Internships, joint papers, technology licensing

### User Study Teams

- **IRB Requirements**: Must be in place before recruitment
- **Participant Compensation**: Budget for fair compensation
- **Diversity**: Ensure representative participant pools
- **Expertise**: Include human factors or HCI expertise if needed

## Multi-Institutional Team Management

### Governance Structure

- **Steering Committee**: PI, Co-PIs, key senior personnel
- **Advisory Board**: External experts, industry partners
- **Decision-Making**: Consensus-based with PI having final authority

### Communication Infrastructure

- **Regular Meetings**: Weekly team calls, monthly steering committee
- **Project Management**: Slack/Teams, shared drives, project management tools
- **Documentation**: Meeting minutes, decision logs, progress reports

### Data Management

- **Shared Protocols**: Common data formats, naming conventions
- **Access Control**: Who can access what data
- **Storage**: Centralized data repository
- **Backup**: Regular backups with off-site storage

### Conflict Resolution

- **Escalation Path**: Team member → PI → Program Officer
- **Authorship Agreements**: Define authorship criteria upfront
- **IP Agreements**: Clear IP ownership and licensing
