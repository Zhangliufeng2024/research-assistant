# Reporting Guidelines for Scientific Studies

## Overview

Reporting guidelines are evidence-based recommendations for what information should be included when reporting specific types of research studies. They provide checklists and flow diagrams to ensure complete, accurate, and transparent reporting, which is essential for readers to assess study validity and for other researchers to replicate the work.

The EQUATOR Network (Enhancing the QUAlity and Transparency Of health Research) maintains a comprehensive library of reporting guidelines. Using appropriate reporting guidelines improves manuscript quality and increases the likelihood of publication acceptance.

## Why Use Reporting Guidelines?

### Benefits

**For authors:**
- Ensures nothing important is forgotten
- Increases acceptance rates
- Improves manuscript organization
- Reduces reviewer requests for additional information

**For readers and reviewers:**
- Enables critical appraisal of study validity
- Facilitates systematic reviews and meta-analyses
- Improves understanding of what was actually done

**For science:**
- Enhances reproducibility
- Reduces research waste
- Improves transparency
- Enables better evidence synthesis

### When to Use

- **During study design**: Many guidelines include protocol versions (e.g., SPIRIT for trial protocols)
- **During manuscript drafting**: Use checklist to ensure all items are covered
- **Before submission**: Verify adherence and often submit checklist with manuscript
- **Many journals require**: Reporting guideline checklists as part of submission

## Major Reporting Guidelines by Study Type

### CONSORT - Randomized Controlled Trials

**Full name:** Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials

**When to use:** Any randomized controlled trial (RCT), including pilot and feasibility trials

**Latest version:** CONSORT 2010 (updated statement)

**Key components:**
- **Checklist**: 25 items covering title, abstract, introduction, methods, results, discussion
- **Flow diagram**: Participant flow through enrollment, allocation, follow-up, and analysis

**Main checklist items:**
1. Title identifies study as randomized trial
2. Structured abstract
3. Scientific background and rationale
4. Specific objectives and hypotheses
5. Trial design description (parallel, crossover, factorial, etc.)
6. Eligibility criteria for participants
7. Settings and locations of data collection
8. Interventions described in sufficient detail for replication
9. Primary and secondary outcomes defined
10. Sample size determination and power calculation
11. Randomization sequence generation
12. Allocation concealment mechanism
13. Blinding implementation
14. Statistical methods
15. Participant flow with reasons for dropouts
16. Recruitment dates and follow-up dates
17. Baseline characteristics table
18. Analysis results for each outcome
19. Harms and adverse events
20. Trial limitations
21. Generalizability
22. Interpretation consistent with results
23. Trial registration number
24. Full protocol access
25. Funding sources

**Extensions for specific designs:**
- CONSORT for cluster randomized trials
- CONSORT for non-inferiority and equivalence trials
- CONSORT for pragmatic trials
- CONSORT for crossover trials
- CONSORT for N-of-1 trials
- CONSORT for stepped wedge designs

**Where to access:** http://www.consort-statement.org/

### STROBE - Observational Studies

**Full name:** Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology

**When to use:** Cohort studies, case-control studies, and cross-sectional studies

**Latest version:** STROBE 2007 (widely adopted standard)

**Key study designs covered:**
- **Cohort**: Follow exposed and unexposed groups forward in time
- **Case-control**: Compare exposure history between cases and controls
- **Cross-sectional**: Measure exposure and outcome simultaneously

**Main checklist items (22 items):**
1. Title and abstract indicate study design
2. Background and rationale
3. Objectives
4. Study design with rationale
5. Setting, locations, and dates
6. Eligibility criteria and selection methods
7. Variables clearly defined (outcomes, exposures, confounders)
8. Data sources and measurement methods
9. Bias management strategies
10. Study size justification
11. Handling of quantitative variables
12. Statistical methods including confounding and interactions
13. Sensitivity analyses
14. Participant flow with reasons for non-participation
15. Descriptive data including follow-up time
16. Outcome data
17. Main results with unadjusted and adjusted estimates
18. Other analyses (subgroups, sensitivity analyses)
19. Key results summary
20. Limitations with potential bias discussion
21. Interpretation and generalizability
22. Funding sources and role

**Extensions:**
- STROBE-ME (Molecular Epidemiology)
- RECORD (Routinely collected health data)
- STROBE-RDS (Respondent-driven sampling)

**Where to access:** https://www.strobe-statement.org/

### PRISMA - Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses

**Full name:** Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses

**When to use:** Systematic reviews with or without meta-analysis

**Latest version:** PRISMA 2020 (significant update)

**Key components:**
- **Checklist**: 27 items covering all sections
- **Flow diagram**: Study selection process

**Main sections:**
1. **Title**: Identify as systematic review/meta-analysis
2. **Abstract**: Structured summary
3. **Introduction**: Rationale and objectives
4. **Methods**:
   - Eligibility criteria
   - Information sources (databases, dates)
   - Search strategy (full strategy for at least one database)
   - Selection process
   - Data collection process
   - Data items extracted
   - Risk of bias assessment
   - Effect measures
   - Synthesis methods
   - Reporting bias assessment
   - Certainty assessment (e.g., GRADE)
5. **Results**:
   - Study selection flow diagram
   - Study characteristics
   - Risk of bias assessment results
   - Synthesis results (meta-analysis if applicable)
   - Reporting biases
   - Certainty of evidence
6. **Discussion**:
   - Limitations
   - Interpretation
   - Implications

**Extensions:**
- PRISMA for Abstracts
- PRISMA for Protocols (PRISMA-P)
- PRISMA for Network Meta-Analyses
- PRISMA for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR)
- PRISMA for Individual Patient Data
- PRISMA for Diagnostic Test Accuracy
- PRISMA for Equity-focused reviews

**Where to access:** http://www.prisma-statement.org/

### SPIRIT - Study Protocols for Clinical Trials

**Full name:** Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials

**When to use:** Protocols for randomized trials and other planned intervention studies

  - Recruitment strategy
  - Allocation and randomization
  - Blinding
  - Data collection methods
  - Data management
  - Statistical methods
  - Monitoring (data monitoring committee)
  - Harms reporting
  - Auditing
- Ethics and dissemination
  - Ethics approval
  - Consent procedures
  - Confidentiality
  - Dissemination plans

**Where to access:** https://www.spirit-statement.org/

## How to Use Reporting Guidelines

### During Study Planning

1. **Identify relevant guideline** based on study design
2. **Review checklist items** that require planning (e.g., randomization, blinding)
3. **Design study** to ensure all required elements will be captured
4. **Consider protocol guidelines** (e.g., SPIRIT for trials)

### During Manuscript Drafting

1. **Download checklist** from guideline website
2. **Work through each item** systematically
3. **Note where each item is addressed** in manuscript (page/line numbers)
4. **Revise manuscript** to include missing items
5. **Use flow diagrams** as appropriate

### Before Submission

1. **Complete formal checklist** with page numbers
2. **Review all items** are adequately addressed
3. **Include checklist** with submission if journal requires
4. **Note guideline adherence** in cover letter or methods

### Example Checklist Entry

```
Item 7: Eligibility criteria for participants, and the settings and locations where the data were collected
Page 6, lines 112-125: "Participants were community-dwelling adults aged 60-85 years with mild cognitive impairment (MCI) as defined by Petersen criteria. Exclusion criteria included dementia diagnosis, major psychiatric disorders, or unstable medical conditions. Recruitment occurred from three memory clinics in Boston, MA, between January 2022 and December 2023."
```

## Creating Flow Diagrams

### CONSORT Flow Diagram

**Four stages:**
1. **Enrollment**: Assessed for eligibility
2. **Allocation**: Randomly assigned to groups
3. **Follow-up**: Received intervention, lost to follow-up
4. **Analysis**: Included in analysis

**Example:**
```

    ↓
Excluded (n=50)
  • Did not meet criteria (n=30)
  • Declined to participate (n=15)
  • Other reasons (n=5)
    ↓
Randomized (n=200)
    ├─────────────────┬─────────────────┐
    ↓                 ↓                 ↓
Allocated to       Allocated to      Allocated to
Intervention A     Intervention B     Control
(n=67)            (n=66)            (n=67)
    ↓                 ↓                 ↓
Lost to follow-up  Lost to follow-up  Lost to follow-up
(n=3)             (n=5)             (n=2)
    ↓                 ↓                 ↓
Analyzed          Analyzed          Analyzed
(n=64)            (n=61)            (n=65)
```

### PRISMA Flow Diagram

**Stages:**
1. **Identification**: Records from databases and registers
2. **Screening**: Records screened, excluded
3. **Included**: Studies included in review and synthesis

**New features in PRISMA 2020:**
- Separate tracking for database and register searches

## Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

### Mistake 1: Not Using Guidelines at All

**Impact:** Missing critical information, lower chance of acceptance

**Solution:** Identify and use appropriate guideline from study planning stage

### Mistake 2: Using Guidelines Only After Manuscript is Complete

**Impact:** May realize key data were not collected or documented

**Solution:** Review guidelines during study design and data collection

### Mistake 3: Incomplete Checklist Completion

**Impact:** Missed items remain unreported

**Solution:** Systematically address every single checklist item

### Mistake 4: Using Outdated Guidelines

**Impact:** Missing recent improvements in reporting standards

**Solution:** Always check for latest version on official guideline website

### Mistake 5: Using Wrong Guideline for Study Design

**Impact:** Important design-specific elements not reported

**Solution:** Carefully match study design to appropriate guideline

### Mistake 6: Not Submitting Checklist When Required

**Impact:** Editorial desk rejection or delays

**Solution:** Check journal submission guidelines and include checklist

### Mistake 7: Generic Reporting Without Specificity

**Impact:** Insufficient detail for replication or appraisal

**Solution:** Provide specific, detailed information for each item

## Venue-Specific Reporting Requirements

### Reporting Standards by Venue Type

| Venue Type | Guideline Use | Transparency Requirements |
|-----------|--------------|---------------------------|
| **Medical journals** | Mandatory (CONSORT, STROBE, etc.) | Checklist required at submission |
| **PLOS/BMC** | Mandatory for study types | Checklist uploaded as supplement |
| **Nature/Science** | Recommended | Methods completeness emphasized |
| **ML conferences** | No formal guidelines | Reproducibility details required |

### ML Conference Reporting Standards

**NeurIPS/ICML/ICLR reproducibility requirements:**
- **Datasets**: Names, versions, access methods, preprocessing
- **Code**: Availability statement; GitHub common
- **Hyperparameters**: All settings reported (learning rate, batch size, etc.)
- **Seeds**: Random seeds for reproducibility
- **Computational resources**: GPUs used, training time
- **Statistical significance**: Error bars, confidence intervals, multiple runs
- **Broader Impact** statement (NeurIPS): Societal implications

**What to include (typically in appendix):**
- Complete hyperparameter settings
- Training details and convergence criteria
- Hardware specifications
- Software versions (PyTorch 2.0, etc.)
- Dataset splits and any preprocessing
- Evaluation metrics and protocols

### Enforcement and Evaluation

**What gets checked:**
- **Medical journals**: Checklist uploaded; adherence statement in Methods; systematic completeness
- **PLOS/BMC**: Mandatory checklists for certain designs; reproducibility emphasized
- **High-impact**: Methods sufficiency for replication (checklist often not required)
- **ML conferences**: Reproducibility checklist (NeurIPS); code availability increasingly expected

**Common issues leading to rejection:**
- Missing required checklists (medical journals)
- Insufficient methods detail for reproduction
- Missing key information (randomization, blinding, power calculation)
- No data/code availability statement when required

**Methods statement examples:**

**Journal (STROBE):**
```
This study followed STROBE reporting guidelines. Checklist provided in Supplement 1.
```

**ML conference (reproducibility):**
```
Code available at github.com/user/project. All hyperparameters in Appendix A.
Training used 4×A100 GPUs (~20 hours). Seeds: {42, 123, 456}.
```

# Engineering & Applied Sciences Reporting Standards

## Overview

Engineering and applied sciences papers follow different reporting conventions than biomedical research. While no single EQUATOR-style guideline governs all engineering fields, major professional societies and standards bodies have established expectations for manuscript quality, reproducibility, and technical rigor. This section covers reporting standards for the major engineering disciplines.

## ASCE Journal Standards

**Applicable to:** Civil, structural, environmental, geotechnical, and construction engineering papers published in ASCE journals.

### Structural Analysis Papers

- **Mesh convergence study** is mandatory for all finite element analyses; report element type, mesh density, and convergence criterion (e.g., <2% change in key output)
- **Boundary conditions** must be fully described with justification (e.g., fixed, pinned, roller; symmetry conditions; far-field distances)
- **Material model validation**: State the constitutive model, cite the source of material parameters, and validate against experimental data or code-specified values
- **Load combinations** must reference the applicable code edition (e.g., ASCE 7-22 Section 2.3)
- **Safety factors** must cite the specific code clause (e.g., LRFD resistance factors per AISC 360-22 Chapter J)

### Required Paper Structure

1. **Problem statement**: Clear definition of the engineering problem and its practical significance
2. **Methodology**: Detailed enough for independent reproduction, including all assumptions
3. **Verification and validation (V&V)**: Code verification (e.g., Method of Manufactured Solutions, patch tests) and validation against experimental data or benchmark problems
4. **Conclusions**: Supported by data, with explicit statements of limitations and applicable ranges

### Formatting Requirements

- Tables must include units in column headers or in a dedicated units row
- Figures must have labeled axes with units (e.g., "Displacement (mm)", "Load (kN)")
- Use SI units as primary; imperial units may be provided in parentheses where convention dictates
- Report numerical results to appropriate precision (typically 3-4 significant figures for engineering quantities)

### ASTM Standards for Structural Testing

When reporting structural testing results, reference the applicable ASTM standard for each test type. The following standards are commonly required:

- **ASTM E8/E8M**: Standard Test Methods for Tension Testing of Metallic Materials — report specimen geometry, strain rate, yield strength (0.2% offset), ultimate tensile strength, elongation, and reduction of area
- **ASTM E9**: Standard Test Methods of Compression Testing of Metallic Materials at Room Temperature — report specimen dimensions, loading rate, and compressive yield strength
- **ASTM E739**: Standard Practice for Statistical Analysis of Linear or Linearized Stress-Life (S-N) and Strain-Life (ε-N) Fatigue Data — **REQUIRED for any fatigue study**; report number of specimens, stress/strain levels, regression parameters, and confidence intervals
- **ASTM E399**: Standard Test Method for Linear-Elastic Plane-Strain Fracture Toughness K_IC of Metallic Materials — report specimen geometry (compact tension or bend), pre-crack length, and K_IC value with validity requirements
- **ASTM E1820**: Standard Test Method for Measurement of Fracture Toughness — report J_IC, crack extension resistance curve (J-R curve), and tearing modulus
- **ASTM C39/C39M**: Standard Test Method for Compressive Strength of Cylindrical Concrete Specimens — report specimen dimensions, curing conditions, loading rate, and compressive strength with age at testing
- **ASTM D695**: Standard Test Method for Compressive Properties of Rigid Plastics — report specimen dimensions, loading rate, and compressive modulus and strength

### ASCE 41-17 Acceptance Criteria

When performing seismic evaluation or retrofit design, report compliance with ASCE 41-17 (Seismic Evaluation and Retrofit of Existing Buildings):

- **Component-level acceptance criteria**: Report acceptance criteria for each structural component (beams, columns, connections, walls) at the applicable performance level
- **Three performance levels**:
  - **Immediate Occupancy (IO)**: Structure remains safe to occupy; limited structural and non-structural damage; interstory drift typically <1%
  - **Life Safety (LS)**: Structure maintains margin against collapse; significant structural damage but stable; interstory drift typically <2%
  - **Collapse Prevention (CP)**: Structure remains standing; severe structural damage; interstory drift typically <4%
- **Modeling parameters**: Report backbone curve parameters (a, b, c, d, e coefficients) for each component type per ASCE 41-17 Tables 10-7 through 10-21
- **Required documentation**: For each performance level, report demand-capacity ratios, plastic hinge locations, mechanism identification, and drift demands

### Fracture Mechanics Reporting

When reporting fracture mechanics studies, ensure the following items are documented:

- [ ] Stress intensity factor (K) calculation method documented (analytical, numerical, or weight function)
- [ ] Crack growth rate (da/dN) vs. stress intensity factor range (ΔK) curves reported with all data points
- [ ] Paris law constants (C, m) fitted and reported with R-squared value and applicable ΔK range
- [ ] Fracture toughness values (K_IC per ASTM E399, J_IC per ASTM E1820) reported with specimen geometry and test conditions
- [ ] Fatigue life prediction methodology described (S-N approach, fracture mechanics approach, or both)
- [ ] S-N curves reported with confidence intervals (typically 95%) and number of specimens per stress level

### Seismic Performance Reporting

When reporting seismic performance evaluation or simulation results, ensure the following items are documented:

- [ ] Ground motion selection and scaling methodology described (e.g., ASCE 7-22 Chapter 16, FEMA P-695 suite, or site-specific hazard analysis)
- [ ] Incremental Dynamic Analysis (IDA) curves reported if applicable, with intensity measure and damage measure definitions
- [ ] Fragility curves reported with lognormal parameters (median and lognormal standard deviation) for each damage state
- [ ] Performance-based assessment following ASCE 41-17 with explicit performance objectives stated
- [ ] Interstory drift ratios reported at each performance level (IO, LS, CP) for all stories
- [ ] Residual drift after seismic events reported, with comparison to repair thresholds

## ASME Journal Standards

**Applicable to:** Mechanical, thermal, manufacturing, and materials engineering papers published in ASME journals.

### Mechanical/Thermal Analysis

- **Convergence study** is mandatory for all computational studies; report mesh/time-step independence with quantitative criteria
- **Uncertainty quantification (UQ)**: Propagate input uncertainties to outputs; report confidence intervals or sensitivity coefficients
- **Experimental papers**: Report instrument calibration procedures, measurement uncertainty per the Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement (GUM, ISO/IEC Guide 98-3), and calibration certificate references

### Simulation Papers

- **Code verification**: Demonstrate that the numerical implementation correctly solves the mathematical model (e.g., Method of Manufactured Solutions, grid convergence index)
- **Validation**: Compare simulation results against experimental data; report error metrics (e.g., relative error, RMSE, R-squared)
- **Software details**: Report software name, version, and relevant solver settings

### Reporting Checklist

- [ ] Convergence study with quantitative criteria reported
- [ ] Material properties cited with source (handbook, manufacturer, experiment)
- [ ] Boundary and initial conditions fully specified
- [ ] Measurement uncertainty reported for all experimental quantities
- [ ] Validation against experiment or benchmark documented
- [ ] Software version and solver settings specified

## IEEE Transactions Standards

**Applicable to:** Electrical, computer, and systems engineering papers published in IEEE journals and conferences.

### Algorithm Papers

- **Computational complexity**: Report Big-O time and space complexity; compare with baseline algorithms
- **Baseline comparisons**: Compare against minimum 3 established methods using standard datasets
- **Statistical significance**: Report p-values or confidence intervals; use multiple random seeds/runs (minimum 5)
- **Reproducibility**: Provide code availability statement; report all hyperparameters

### Systems Papers

- **Architecture diagram**: Include a clear system architecture or block diagram
- **Performance benchmarks**: Report throughput, latency, accuracy, and resource utilization
- **Ablation study**: Systematically remove or vary key components to demonstrate their contribution
- **Scalability analysis**: Report performance as a function of system size or load

### Dataset Reporting

- **Source**: Cite the dataset origin (paper, repository, or collection protocol)
- **Size**: Report number of samples, features, and classes
- **Preprocessing**: Describe all data cleaning, normalization, and transformation steps
- **Train/test split**: Report split ratio, stratification method, and whether splits are random or temporal
- **Cross-validation**: Report k-fold or other validation strategy

### Reporting Checklist

- [ ] Computational complexity stated and compared with baselines
- [ ] Minimum 3 baseline methods compared
- [ ] Statistical significance reported (p-values, confidence intervals, or error bars)
- [ ] Architecture or system diagram included
- [ ] Ablation study on key components performed
- [ ] Dataset source, size, and preprocessing documented
- [ ] Code availability statement included
- [ ] All hyperparameters and random seeds reported

## Chinese GB/JGJ Standards Reporting

**When research involves Chinese national standards (GB) or industry standards (JGJ), the following must be reported with exactitude.**

### Mandatory Reporting Items

- **Exact code edition year**: Always report the full standard number with year (e.g., "GB 50011-2010", NOT just "GB 50011"; "JGJ 3-2010", NOT just "JGJ 3"). Chinese codes are periodically revised, and design values change between editions.
- **Material design values with clause reference**: Report the specific clause or table from which design values are taken (e.g., "f_y = 345 MPa per Table 3.4.1-1 of GB 50017-2017" or "C30 concrete with f_ck = 20.1 MPa per Table 4.1.3-1 of GB 50010-2010")
- **Seismic design parameters**: Report site class (I-IV), seismic design group (first/second/third), fortification intensity, and basic seismic acceleration per GB 50011-2010 Chapter 3
- **Load combinations**: Report specific combination factors per GB 50009-2012 Section 3.2, distinguishing between fundamental and accidental combinations
- **Safety factor gamma_0**: Report the structural importance coefficient per GB 50068-2018 Table 3.2.1 based on structural importance level (first/second/third level)

### Example Reporting Statement

> The steel frame was designed in accordance with GB 50011-2010 for seismic fortification intensity 7 (design basic acceleration 0.10g), site class II, seismic design group I. Steel Q345B was specified for columns (f_y = 345 MPa per Table 3.4.1-1 of GB 50017-2017) and Q235B for beams (f_y = 235 MPa). Load combinations followed GB 50009-2012 Section 3.2, with structural importance coefficient gamma_0 = 1.0 per GB 50068-2018 for second-class importance structures.

## Environmental Engineering Reporting (Carbon/Energy/HVAC Focus)

### Greenhouse Gas (GHG) Reporting (ISO 14064)

- **Organizational boundary**: Define operational or equity control approach per ISO 14064-1
- **Scope delineation**: Clearly separate Scope 1 (direct combustion, refrigerant leakage), Scope 2 (purchased electricity, district heating/cooling), and Scope 3 (embodied carbon, construction, transportation) per GHG Protocol
- **Emission factors**: Report with source, year, and uncertainty range (e.g., "0.5811 kg CO2/kWh per China's national grid average, 2022")
- **GWP time horizon**: State the time horizon (typically 100-year) and source of GWP values (AR5 or AR6)
- **Carbon intensity**: Report per functional unit (e.g., kg CO2e/m2/year for buildings, kg CO2e/kWh for energy systems)
- **Verification**: Report third-party verification status if applicable

### Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) Studies

Must comply with **ISO 14040:2006** and **ISO 14044:2006**. Report:

- **Functional unit**: Clearly defined and justified (e.g., "1 m2 of wall assembly with 50-year service life", "1 kWh of delivered heating energy")
- **System boundary**: Cradle-to-gate, cradle-to-grave, or cradle-to-cradle; justify inclusion/exclusion of processes
- **Allocation method**: Mass, economic, or system expansion; justify choice per ISO 14044 Section 4.3.5
- **LCIA method**: Specify the impact assessment method (e.g., ReCiPe 2016, CML-IA, TRACI) and midpoint/endpoint indicators used
- **Data sources**: Primary data vs. background database (e.g., ecoinvent, GaBi); report database version
- **Sensitivity analysis**: Test key assumptions and data quality

### Building Energy Simulation Reporting

Must comply with **ASHRAE Standards** and report the following:

- **Simulation software**: Name, version, and validation status (e.g., EnergyPlus 23.1, DeST, TRNSYS)
- **Weather data**: Source (CTSWDB for China, TMY3 for US, IWEC for international), location, typical vs. design day
- **Building geometry**: Floor area (gross/net), number of stories, orientation, window-to-wall ratio (WWR)
- **Envelope properties**: U-values for walls/roof/floor/windows (W/m2-K), thermal mass, air tightness (ACH50)
- **HVAC system**: System type (VAV, FCU, VRV, split), COP/EER/SEER, part-load curves
- **Internal loads**: Lighting (W/m2), equipment (W/m2), occupancy (people/m2), schedule
- **Simulation settings**: Timestep, convergence criteria, solar distribution algorithm
- **Calibration**: If comparing to measured data, report CV-RMSE (<=15% monthly, <=30% hourly per ASHRAE Guideline 14) and NMBE (<=5% monthly)

**Required Output Metrics:**
- Energy Use Intensity (EUI): kWh/m2/year or kBtu/ft2/year
- Peak heating/cooling load: W/m2
- Carbon intensity: kgCO2e/m2/year (operational carbon)

### HVAC System Reporting

- **System specifications**: Type, capacity, efficiency ratings (COP, EER, SEER, IPLV)
- **Control strategy**: Setpoints, schedules, demand-controlled ventilation parameters
- **Thermal comfort**: PMV/PPD indices per ASHRAE 55
- **Indoor air quality**: CO2, PM2.5, TVOC concentrations per ASHRAE 62.1
- **Energy performance**: Comparison with baseline system per ASHRAE 90.1
- **Economic analysis**: Payback period, life cycle cost (LCC), net present value (NPV)

### Energy Audit Reporting (ASHRAE Level 1/2/3)

- **Level 1 (Walk-through)**: Energy cost analysis, preliminary savings estimates, low-cost recommendations
- **Level 2 (Energy Survey)**: Detailed energy analysis, recommended improvements with cost estimates, M&V plan
- **Level 3 (Investment-Grade)**: Hourly simulation, detailed engineering analysis, capital-intensive recommendations with financial models
- **Benchmarking**: Compare building performance against Energy Star or LEED benchmarks

### Carbon Footprint Reporting (GHG Protocol)

**Product/System Carbon Footprint:**
```
CF = Sum(Mi x EFi) + Sum(Ej x EFj) + Sum(Dk x EFk)

Where:
  CF = Carbon Footprint (kg CO2 eq)
  Mi = Mass of material i (kg)
  EFi = Emission factor for material i (kg CO2 eq/kg)
  Ej = Energy consumption j (kWh or MJ)
  EFj = Emission factor for energy source j
  Dk = Distance for transport k (km)
  EFk = Emission factor for transport mode k (kg CO2 eq/kg-km)
```

**Building Carbon Accounting Template:**
```
Scope 1: [value] tCO2e/year (on-site combustion, refrigerant leakage)
Scope 2: [value] tCO2e/year (using [grid emission factor] kgCO2/kWh)
Scope 3: [value] tCO2e/year (embodied carbon, construction, end-of-life)
Total: [value] tCO2e/year
EUI: [value] kWh/m2/year
Carbon Intensity: [value] kgCO2e/m2/year
```

## AI/ML for Engineering Reporting

**When machine learning methods are applied to engineering problems, the following must be reported in addition to standard IEEE/ML conference requirements.**

### Model Development

- **Architecture diagram**: Include a clear figure showing model architecture with layer dimensions
- **Training details**: Report epochs, learning rate (initial and schedule), optimizer, batch size, loss function, and early stopping criteria
- **Hardware**: Report GPU/CPU model, memory, and total training time
- **Software framework**: Report framework (PyTorch, TensorFlow) and version

### Dataset

- **Source**: Describe data origin (simulation, experiment, field measurement, or hybrid)
- **Size**: Report number of samples, input features, and output dimensions
- **Preprocessing**: Describe normalization, outlier handling, missing data treatment, and any augmentation
- **Train/validation/test split**: Report ratios and whether splits are random, temporal, or spatial

### Evaluation

- **Baseline comparisons**: Compare against minimum 3 baselines, including at least one physics-based/traditional method
- **Ablation study**: Systematically remove or vary key model components (attention heads, layers, features)
- **Computational cost comparison**: Compare inference time and training cost with traditional engineering methods
- **Physical consistency checks**: Verify that model outputs satisfy physical constraints (e.g., equilibrium satisfaction, energy conservation, mass balance, non-negative stresses, bounded strain)
- **Generalization**: Test on out-of-distribution data or different scales/geometries not seen during training

### Reporting Checklist

- [ ] Architecture diagram with dimensions included
- [ ] Training hyperparameters fully reported
- [ ] Dataset source, size, and preprocessing documented
- [ ] Minimum 3 baseline comparisons (including physics-based method)
- [ ] Ablation study on key components performed
- [ ] Computational cost comparison with traditional methods
- [ ] Physical consistency checks reported
- [ ] Generalization testing on unseen configurations
- [ ] Code and data availability statement
